# Tender Clarity (working title)

Turn one confusing tender document into a clear, traceable preparation plan for a small contractor.

## The Unique Kernel
A contractor can see what a tender asks for, why each requirement matters, and where it appears in the source document. The tool connects that understanding to concrete preparation steps, instead of offering a generic tender summary.

## Who It's For
A small contractor with limited time and procurement support who is considering a tender and needs to understand its requirements, prepare a complete submission, and assess basic costs. The learner brings firsthand experience pursuing tenders for a small cleaning business in South Africa.

## The Core Loop
For one tender, the contractor uploads the document, reviews a plain-language explanation of the opportunity and its critical requirements with source references, follows an actionable preparation checklist, and enters basic cost estimates in a simple worksheet.

## Inspiration & Identity
No specific app or service inspiration identified. The experience should be clear and understandable to a nontechnical small-business owner.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
The learner has spent years running a small cleaning business and pursuing tenders, and has experienced how long, complicated documents can hide requirements and make preparation difficult. They want to help smaller contractors understand opportunities and prepare in a disciplined way, without promising that the tool can make them win.

## What "Working" Looks Like
In a short demonstration, a contractor uploads one tender and gets a useful view of what it is for, eligibility requirements, deadlines, evaluation criteria, mandatory documents, scope requirements, potential disqualification items, cost inputs, and unclear or missing information. Important findings can be traced to the tender. The contractor gets a practical checklist and can enter basic costs to see a simple cost/viability calculation. A nontechnical user can understand where to start and what to do next.

## The POC Boundary
Analyze one tender document and turn its contents into a plain-language, source-traceable brief and actionable checklist. Include critical requirements, dates, evaluation criteria, potential disqualification items, and cost information the contractor needs to calculate. Include a simple worksheet for contractor-entered cost estimates and a basic cost/viability calculation. Keep the prototype understandable to a nontechnical user and avoid claims about winning or predicting outcomes.

## Later
External web research such as previous tender versions or award information; richer financial analysis; exploring delivery through governments, municipalities, procurement organizations, or small-business support programs.

## Explicitly Cut
- Web research in the prototype, to keep the results reliable and clearly separate from the tender itself.
- Claims that the tool increases the chance of winning by a specific percentage or guarantees an outcome, because outcomes depend on competitors and other unknown factors.
- Government or institutional deployment in the first build, because the prototype should first prove the core tender-to-preparation-plan journey.
